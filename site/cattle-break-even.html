Put your own numbers in. This works out what an animal has cost you by the time it leaves the yard, what you need to get for it to break even, and how that compares with where the Alberta market actually is.

Information only — this is NOT investment advice

Everything on this page is general information to help you understand your own costs. It is not financial, investment, tax or marketing advice, and it is not a recommendation to buy or sell anything at any time.

Nothing here predicts prices. The historical section shows what happened in the past. Past patterns do not tell you what the market will do next, and cattle markets are driven by weather, feed, herd size, trade and currency in ways no chart accounts for.

Prices shown are Statistics Canada monthly provincial averages for Alberta, published to 2026-07. They are a reference point, not a quote, and not what your cattle will bring on any given day. Talk to your lender, your accountant and your marketing advisor before making decisions.

Read this before using the chart above. Statistics Canada publishes one feeder price for the whole province, averaged across every weight sold that month. It has no weight-class breakdown at all. In the autumn that average fills up with lighter calves, which always sell for more per hundredweight than heavy feeders. We measured that effect - the light-cattle premium alone swings 10.6% through the year, which is the same size as the pattern shown here. So this chart cannot tell you whether the same animal is worth more in September. A weight-specific feeder series answers that question; this one cannot.

Where these numbers come from

  • Prices are Statistics Canada table 32-10-0077, Farm product prices, crops and livestock, Alberta, monthly. Free and public. Latest month available is 2026-07.
  • Cattle are quoted in dollars per hundredweight and grain in dollars per tonne. Those units come from the data itself, not from an assumption.
  • The seasonal figures use only complete calendar years, so a part-finished year cannot bias a month against its own average.
  • These are provincial averages across all sales in the month. They are not sale-barn prices, not weight-specific, and not what any individual lot brought.
  • A live, weight-specific feed is not connected yet. When it is, the market section updates from it and the historical section stays as it is.
